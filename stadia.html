Stadia Store Redesign

A refreshed storefront experience, enabling users to find & buy games they will love faster.

CompanyGoogle
Duration7 months
ShippedFebruary 2022
RoleUX Lead

Context

An opportunity to level up.

As the game catalog grew, the store needed to evolve to accommodate an increasing number of titles, bundles, and add‑ons, while keeping discovery and purchasing effortless.

Supporting screenshot
Problems

More games, more choices.

The outdated store came with its own set of issues:

  • Poor scalability — the layout and navigation weren't built for a rapidly expanding library.
  • Unintuitive purchase flow — evaluating and buying wasn't clear, which hurt conversion.
  • Hard to discover — limited filtering and personalization made it difficult to find the right title.
  • Mixed pricing signals — a separate membership pricing model effectively doubled every price shown.
  • Unfriendly to mobile — the interface wasn't fully optimized for smaller screens.
Challenge

Transform an overwhelming store experience into an intuitive, engaging storefront that guides people to their next favorite game.

D‑pad first

Prioritize navigation and interactions that feel natural for controller users.

Scalable & flexible

A forward‑thinking approach so the storefront readily adapts to new content and needs.

Simplicity & clarity

Minimize complexity and cognitive load with clear, concise information.

Consistency & familiarity

Adhere to established platform conventions for a predictable experience.

Growing pains — Storefront

Pinpointed issues.

As new titles were added, people struggled to navigate the expanding collection, and the homepage's limited space made it hard to showcase content well.

  • Difficulty navigating a growing catalog
  • Limited discoverability of new or relevant games
  • Lack of clear organization and categorization
  • Overwhelming presentation of information
Before / after comparison

Empowering people with filtered search.

Filtered search let people refine by genre, price, and platform — turning an overwhelming catalog into a targeted, efficient search for exactly what they wanted.

Measuring success

12%

Increase in SKU sales across the store in the month after launch.

Retrospective

Project takeaways.

  • Influence through input — designing d‑pad first forces clear focus states and optimizes for directional navigation.
  • The power of passive discovery — curated homepage content exposes people to games they wouldn't have actively searched for.
  • Scaling with two columns — a simple two‑column layout accommodated a growing library without sacrificing clarity.
  • State‑based interactions — using state changes to convey clickability reduced the need for explicit visual cues.
